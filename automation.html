What I automate

The target is expensive repetitive work, not technology for its own sake.

These are examples of processes worth examining. They are not claims about completed client projects.

Common automation targets

If people repeat the same digital steps over and over, there may be room to remove labor, delay, or error.

Reports and spreadsheets

Recurring reports, Excel files, CSV exports, formulas, cleanup steps, manual filtering, and weekly spreadsheet rebuilds.

Documents and PDFs

PDF processing, OCR, form review, attachment handling, email classification, document routing, and extraction into usable data.

Data transfer between systems

Information copied between apps, compared across exports, checked against a database, or prepared for another department.

Exception review

Processes where most records are routine, but employees spend hours finding the small number that need human attention.

Internal utilities

Small tools, dashboards, review queues, scripts, background jobs, and internal applications built around how the work happens.

AI-assisted tasks

Classification, summarization, text analysis, and judgment-heavy review where AI makes sense alongside rules and human oversight.

Practical automation

Not every automation needs AI.

Some of the best automation is boring in a good way: a reliable script, a clean integration, a scheduled report processor, or an internal tool that prevents employees from doing the same task hundreds of times.

AI is useful when the work involves language, classification, summarization, or judgment-like interpretation. It should earn its place in the workflow.
