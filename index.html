Problem recognition

Processes worth examining often look like this.

If the same digital task happens again and again, there may be an opportunity to reduce the human time required to complete it.

Recurring report review

Managers or employees scan large reports looking for the same exceptions, mismatches, or status changes.

Spreadsheet rebuilding

Someone copies data from exports, cleans columns, applies formulas, and rebuilds the same workbook every week.

Manual system transfer

Information is read in one system and re-entered into another because the tools do not talk to each other.

Document processing

PDFs, forms, emails, or attachments need to be classified, checked, summarized, routed, or converted into usable data.

Data reconciliation

Staff compare two exports, lists, or systems to find differences, missing records, duplicate entries, or bad values.

Repeated exception handling

A person spends hours filtering out the routine cases just to find the few records that actually need attention.

Engineering judgment

Not every process should be automated.

Automation makes sense when the process is repeated often enough, consistent enough, and valuable enough that reducing the manual work is worth the build effort.

Some processes are too rare, too variable, too low-value, or too dependent on human judgment to justify a custom tool. Saying no to those projects is part of building responsibly.

Good candidates

  • Repeated frequently
  • Consumes meaningful staff or management time
  • Uses information software can access
  • Has describable rules or patterns
  • Creates delays, errors, or bottlenecks

Weak candidates

  • Happens only once in a while
  • Changes completely every time
  • Costs more to automate than it returns
  • Relies almost entirely on human judgment
  • Cannot provide reliable data access
